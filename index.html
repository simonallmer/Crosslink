          Rulebook

          “The dictionary defines words only in terms of other words. What matters is not the words themselves which are a collection of mostly arbitrary sounds, but the relationships between one word to the next.”

          Tim Berners-Lee, CERN, 1994

          The board

          Nine words stand in the cells of a three-by-three lattice. Between them, in the gutters, stand the connections that join them. Every gutter is a sentence: the word at the tail, the connection, the word at the head.

          What you can see

          Every connection on the board is shown from the start. Nothing is hidden: the whole network is there to be read, and you can work out any square in it from where you are sitting.

          You may write in any square, in any order. The squares a placed word already touches are marked, because that is usually where the next one comes easiest — but nothing is locked, and if you can see a word in the far corner you may set it down at once.

          Entering a word

          Click a lit square and type. Each letter has its own line, so you can count what the square wants. Enter word sets it down; Take it back lifts it again. Nothing is final, and a square you take back keeps any mark it earned.

          Help, and what it costs

          Reveal a letter seats a letter you did not type, and marks the square as partly solved. Reveal the word marks it as given, not solved. The board keeps the count and reads it back to you at the close.

          Pressing S gives up the whole board at once, and says so in the report.

          The switch

          Error check is on to begin with: a wrong word goes red the moment you enter it. Turn it off and nothing on the board ever contradicts you — a word you set down is simply taken, and you find out at the close. The board sounds different that way too: with the check off it will not tell you in a noise what it is refusing to tell you on the page.

          The two windows

          Clicking the sentences in the clue panel opens the Written form, which holds every sentence the board has surfaced, whole or half-known. Clicking a word you have placed opens the Lexicon, which holds its senses — usually more than one, and usually the second one is the one the board is using.

          The hunt

          No word is ever set in two boards. The Word List is the whole quarry, and every board takes nine words out of it for good.

          Difficulty

          Each board carries one, two or three stars. The count comes from the words on it: how ordinary they are, and how many of them are being used in a second sense. A board of plain facts earns one star. A board where half the words are wearing two coats earns three.
